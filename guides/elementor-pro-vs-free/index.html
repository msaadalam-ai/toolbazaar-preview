Elementor free vs Elementor Pro: what you actually get

The free plugin is a real page builder, not a trial. Pro is a second plugin that adds the pieces a business website needs. Here is where the line sits.

Updated 20 October 2026 · Written by AI Web Tools

Order on WhatsAppSee pricing
Short answer

The free Elementor plugin gives you the drag-and-drop editor and a set of basic widgets, which is enough for a simple page or two. Elementor Pro adds the parts a business site needs: the Theme Builder for headers, footers and post layouts, the Form widget, the Popup Builder, the WooCommerce Builder, the Loop Grid, dynamic content, motion effects and custom CSS. If you only need a one-page site with text, images and a button, stay on free. If you need forms, a custom header or a shop, you need Pro.

What the free plugin includes

Free Elementor installs from the WordPress plugin directory and stays free. You get the visual editor with containers, responsive controls for desktop, tablet and mobile, global colours and fonts in Site Settings, the blank Canvas page template, a library of free templates, and the basic widgets: heading, text, image, video, button, icon box, image carousel, basic gallery, tabs, accordion, counter, testimonial, Google Maps and similar.

That is enough to build a clean brochure page inside whatever header and footer your theme provides.

Three cases where free is enough

  • A personal page or a one-page profile with text, photos and a WhatsApp button.
  • A brochure site where the theme's own header and footer are fine and you already use a separate form plugin.
  • Learning. Build your first few sites on free; you will know exactly which Pro feature you are missing when you hit it.

Three cases where you need Pro

  • You want your own header and footer, or a blog and post layout that does not look like the theme's default.
  • You need forms that do something after submit, without stacking extra plugins.
  • You run a WooCommerce store and want product and checkout pages that match the rest of the site.

The free-plus-add-ons route

Free add-on packs can fill some of the gaps, and for a hobby site that is fine. The cost is weight and risk: each pack loads its own CSS and scripts, has its own update cycle, and some are abandoned. For a client site, one Pro plugin from the same developer as the core builder is simpler to maintain than four add-ons from four developers.

Is Elementor free forever?

Yes. The free plugin is published on WordPress.org under the GPL and has stayed free since 2016. Pro is a separate paid plugin that runs on top of it.

Can I switch from free to Pro without rebuilding?

Yes. Pro installs alongside the free plugin and unlocks the extra widgets and builders. Your existing pages are untouched.

Does Pro replace my theme?

Not exactly. With the Theme Builder you design the header, footer and templates yourself, so a light theme such as Elementor's Hello theme is all you need underneath.

Does the free version have a contact form?

No. The Form widget is a Pro feature. On free you need a separate form plugin.
